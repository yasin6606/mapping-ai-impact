Europe: location shapes impact

Supporting questions 3–5 · Distribution · Electricity mix · Carbon comparison

The same megawatt-hour does not have the same carbon cost everywhere. This page compares where data centres cluster in Europe and what the national electricity mix implies for emissions.

3. Where are data centres concentrated?

Facility counts describe infrastructure distribution, not electricity use. Public directories (e.g. Data Center Map / Cloudscene-style listings) consistently place the United Kingdom, Germany, France and the Netherlands among the densest European markets. Ireland hosts fewer sites but a high share of hyperscale capacity relative to its size.

4. Electricity mix in key countries

Using national generation shares (Ember / Our World in Data style data), countries with many data centres differ sharply: France is nuclear-heavy; Norway and Sweden are dominated by hydro and other renewables; Germany and the Netherlands still rely more on fossil generation in residual mix terms; Ireland’s mix has shifted with wind growth but remains exposed to gas.

5. Same electricity, different carbon

If a data centre draws 1 TWh in a high-intensity grid versus a low-intensity one, estimated CO₂ can differ by a factor of several times. This is a scenario based on national average intensity—not measured emissions from a specific facility. Operators also buy certificates and PPAs that change the contractual footprint.

Why location matters

  • Grid intensity (gCO₂e/kWh) multiplies every TWh of demand.
  • Cool climates can reduce cooling electricity (and water), improving PUE.
  • Policy, interconnection queues and renewable build-out change the outlook year by year.
