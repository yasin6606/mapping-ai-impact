Water & cooling

Environmental side of data-centre heat rejection

Every TWh of electricity that is not free-air cooled requires water somewhere — either on site or at the power plant. As data-centre load grows from 416 TWh (2024) toward 946 TWh (Base 2030), water demand scales with it.

Why water follows electricity

PUE measures facility overhead. A PUE of 1.41 means roughly 29% of site power is cooling and other non-IT load. Evaporative cooling turns part of that energy into water consumption. Published water-use effectiveness (WUE) for large operators typically sits between about 0.12 and 0.9 litres per kWh of IT energy, depending on climate and design.

Indirect water

Power plants that generate the electricity also consume or withdraw water. On many grids the water embedded in electricity exceeds on-site cooling. Therefore the environmental water footprint of AI is not only a cooling-design problem; it is also a grid-mix problem. Regions with high renewable share and dry cooling reduce both direct and indirect demand.

Link to energy scenarios

  • Base 2030 (946 TWh) roughly doubles 2024 water pressure if WUE stays constant.
  • Lift-Off (1 264 TWh) multiplies the same pressure further.
  • High Efficiency (792 TWh) and lower PUE cut the water bill even before cooling technology improves.
