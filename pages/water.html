Water Footprint

Cooling, evaporation and the hidden thirst of AI

Data centres need water to reject heat. Operator reports show large absolute volumes; independent estimates suggest that inference for a single large model can approach the drinking-water needs of millions of people each year.

What the numbers say

Water-use effectiveness (WUE) reported by operators ranges roughly from 0.12 to 0.91 litres per kWh of IT energy. Climate, cooling design and whether the figure includes only on-site evaporation or also the water embedded in electricity generation explain most of the spread.

Why water is hard to count

On-site cooling water is only part of the story. The electricity that powers servers also requires water at power plants. Studies that include this indirect use often find totals several times higher than the cooling-only numbers published by companies. Location matters: a facility on a clean, cool grid with free-air cooling can have a far smaller footprint than one in a hot, water-stressed region relying on evaporative towers.
