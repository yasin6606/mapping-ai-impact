Environment & carbon

Emissions intensity · scenario risk · grid context

Carbon impact of AI is the product of electricity volume and the carbon intensity of the grids that supply it. IEA scenarios show how large the volume term can become; Ember generation data shows how clean or dirty each country’s mix is.

Volume risk: four 2030 pathways

Using the IEA annex, world data-centre electricity in 2030 ranges from 669 TWh (Headwinds) to 1 264 TWh (Lift-Off). Base sits at 946 TWh; High Efficiency at 792 TWh. The gap between High Efficiency and Lift-Off is almost 500 TWh — enough to change national emissions trajectories if supplied by fossil plant.

Intensity: the other half of the equation

Ember’s yearly generation series shows large differences in gCO₂e/kWh across countries. A data centre on a hydro- or nuclear-heavy grid emits far less per TWh than one on a coal-heavy grid. Training emissions estimates (tens of thousands of tonnes CO₂e for a single frontier model) sit on top of this continuous operational load.

Environmental takeaway

  • Energy volume is set by capacity growth, utilisation and PUE — IEA scenarios quantify the range.
  • Carbon intensity is set by the local power mix — Ember data make country differences visible.
  • Policy levers that cut volume (efficiency, workload placement) and intensity (clean power PPAs, siting) both matter; neither alone is enough under Lift-Off demand.
