Carbon & Training

Emissions from model development and data-centre power

Training one frontier model can release tens of thousands of tonnes of CO₂ equivalent. Scaling that activity — and the continuous inference that follows — is already visible in national electricity and emissions statistics.

Training footprints

Estimates vary with assumptions about hardware utilisation, electricity carbon intensity and system boundaries. The Stanford AI Index 2026 cites an estimate of roughly 72,816 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent for training Grok 4 — many times higher than earlier large models. Inference emissions now dominate the long-run total because they continue for the life of the deployed system.

Grid and policy context

Where new data centres connect to fossil-heavy grids — or bring their own gas generation — the carbon intensity of AI can rise even while model efficiency improves. Clean power purchase agreements, better utilisation, and smaller specialised models for routine tasks are the main levers available today. Transparent reporting of both training and inference energy remains incomplete across the industry.
