New section · Time and distribution

Trends & distributions in AI’s energy context

Five views of change, variation and relationships · Global demand and European electricity mix

A single average can hide important differences. These visualizations follow data-centre electricity over time and compare how electricity generation is distributed across ten European countries. The country-level charts use the same 2024 electricity-mix data already presented in Mix charts; the coal ridgeline compares the available 2020 and 2024 observations.

01 Time series02 Box plot03 Distribution04 Scatter05 Ridgeline

01 · Mandatory · Time-dependent phenomenon

Data-centre electricity demand is increasing

Global data-centre electricity consumption and the IEA-style Base outlook, in terawatt-hours (TWh). The dashed segment marks the 2030 projection; it is not an AI-only estimate.

Values are the rounded figures already used on the project’s Energy page: 2020 269, 2023 361, 2024 416, 2025 485 TWh, and Base case 2030 950 TWh. Historical/recent values and projection are distinguished.

02 · Mandatory · Distribution

How widely does low-carbon generation vary?

Box plot of the low-carbon share (wind + solar + hydropower + nuclear) across the ten countries in the 2024 sample. Dots show each country’s individual observation.

Country percentages are calculated from the existing 2024 electricity-mix values. The box spans the interquartile range; the centre line is the median; whiskers show the sample minimum and maximum.

03 · Distribution · Country-level spread

Fossil generation shares differ across countries

A jitter/strip plot of fossil generation (gas + coal + oil) as a percentage of each country’s total electricity generation in 2024. Each point represents one country.

Percentages are derived from the same 2024 country totals as the Mix charts. This describes national electricity systems, not the electricity consumed by AI facilities.

04 · Relationship · Scatter plot

Low-carbon and fossil shares form contrasting profiles

Each point is a country. The horizontal axis shows low-carbon generation share; the vertical axis shows fossil generation share. The two measures need not sum to 100% because bioenergy and other categories are treated separately.

Both axes are calculated from the 2024 electricity-mix data. The chart is descriptive and does not establish a causal relationship.

05 · Extra · Ridgeline plot (time + distribution)

Coal generation distributions shifted between 2020 and 2024

Ridgeline density curves compare the distribution of coal generation across eight European countries in 2020 and 2024. Each ridge summarizes country-level observations for one year; the curves are smoothed representations, not additional measurements.

Exact 2020 and 2024 coal TWh values are copied from the existing Mix charts data. Gaussian kernel density is used only to display the distribution. Countries included: Germany, Poland, Italy, Netherlands, United Kingdom, Spain, France and Ireland.

How to interpret this section

  • The global time series is about all data centres; it must not be read as AI-only electricity use.
  • Country charts show national electricity-generation context. They are not measurements of individual data centres.
  • Box, strip, scatter and ridgeline plots reveal variation that a single average can hide.
  • Country shares are derived from the same source values used by the existing Mix charts. No original project data or existing visualizations have been replaced.

Data sources & method

  • Global data-centre demand: rounded IEA-linked figures already documented on the Energy page.
  • European electricity generation: electricity-mix.csv (Ember / Our World in Data), the same 2024 values used on the Mix charts page.
  • Coal time comparison: existing 2020 and 2024 country values in pages/charts.html.
  • Charts: D3.js v7. All country shares are calculated in the browser from the displayed source values.
