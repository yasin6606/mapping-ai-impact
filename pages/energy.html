Energy demand

Supporting questions 1–2 · Growth · AI share · Uncertainty

Global data-centre electricity is rising fast. AI is a major driver of new load, but published “AI share” figures depend on definitions and are uncertain. We separate total data-centre demand from AI-related estimates.

1. How has consumption changed—and where is it headed?

IEA-linked figures put world data-centre electricity near 415–485 TWh in the mid-2020s (about 1.5% of global electricity). Growth has been double-digit. In the IEA Base case, demand roughly doubles to around 945–950 TWh by 2030 (~3% of global electricity). Other cases span roughly 670–1 260 TWh depending on efficiency, AI uptake and grid constraints.

2. What about AI’s share—and how uncertain is it?

Total data-centre demand is better measured than the slice that is “AI”. Estimates of AI-focused load vary because of:

  • Definitions — training only vs training + inference; accelerated servers only vs whole facility.
  • Boundaries — IT power vs full site (PUE); on-site generation vs grid.
  • Disclosure — operators rarely publish AI-specific kWh.

IEA work highlights that accelerated servers (strongly linked to AI) grow much faster than conventional servers—on the order of ~30% per year in the Base case versus ~9% for conventional. AI-focused facilities can account for a large share of growth even when their share of the stock is still developing. Treat any single “X% is AI” headline as uncertain.

How to read the numbers

  • Prefer ranges and scenarios over one precise AI percentage.
  • Separate “all data centres” from “AI-related / accelerated servers”.
  • Location and grid mix change the environmental meaning of every TWh (see Europe page).

Regional scale

In recent IEA regional breakdowns, the United States accounts for the largest share of data-centre electricity, followed by China and Europe. Absolute TWh—not facility counts alone—drive system impact.
