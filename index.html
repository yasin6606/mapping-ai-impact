The Impact of AI on the Environment and Energy Consumption

Group Insights · Yasin · Bahar · Pantea · Ferdaws

Data centres used 416 TWh in 2024. Under IEA Base assumptions that rises to about 946 TWh by 2030. This site maps electricity demand and environmental pressure with open data.

1. Project description

Group Insights documents two linked effects of AI:

  • Energy consumption — how much electricity data centres draw (total, IT, by region and by scenario).
  • Environment — water for cooling, grid carbon intensity, and scenario risk if demand follows Lift-Off instead of High Efficiency.

Primary numbers come from the IEA Energy and AI data annex (installed capacity, PUE, load factor, electricity by segment and region, four scenarios to 2030–2035). Grid intensity context uses Ember yearly generation statistics.

What the scenarios mean

  • Base — continuation of recent growth; about 946 TWh in 2030.
  • Lift-Off — faster AI and cloud expansion; about 1 264 TWh.
  • High Efficiency — stronger efficiency and workload discipline; about 792 TWh.
  • Headwinds — constrained build-out; about 669 TWh.

Data sources

  • IEA data annex (Energy and AI) — capacity, PUE, load factor, TWh by world / region / segment
  • Ember yearly generation — electricity mix and emissions intensity by country
  • IEA Key Questions on Energy and AI
  • Stanford AI Index 2026
